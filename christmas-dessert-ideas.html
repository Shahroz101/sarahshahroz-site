22 Christmas Dessert Ideas for Your Holiday Table

From a chocolate yule log to snowman cake pops on a gold stand — 22 desserts, showstoppers and no-bake treats alike, to fill out a holiday table.

2026-10-26 · 8 min read

Christmas dinner gets all the planning, but it is usually the dessert table that people remember the next day.

A good lineup does not need to be complicated.

Something chocolate, something fruity, and a few bite-sized treats that are easy to grab between conversations will cover it.

The best spreads mix one or two showstoppers with a handful of simple things made ahead, so nobody is stuck in the kitchen while everyone else is in the living room.

This roundup runs from a classic bûche de Noël and sticky toffee pudding to no-bake cheesecake cups and chocolate-covered pretzels.

Here are 22 Christmas dessert ideas to help you build out your holiday table this year.

1. Shape a Pavlova into a Wreath

A meringue baked in a ring instead of a round reads as a wreath the moment it hits the table.

This one is piped in soft peaks, dusted with powdered sugar, and topped with whipped cream, sugared cranberries, and sprigs of rosemary that stand in for pine.

It sits on a gray stoneware plate with fairy lights glowing behind it.

Bake the ring the day before and leave it in the turned-off oven overnight. Fill it only an hour before serving so the shell stays crisp.

2. Turn Brownies into Santa Hats

A strawberry, a swirl of whipped cream, and a dot on top is all it takes.

Fudgy brownie squares on a wood board here each wear a whole strawberry set point-up in a ring of cream, with a small cream pom on the tip.

Hull the strawberries flat so they stand straight, and pipe the cream right before serving so the brim does not slump.

3. Bake a Chocolate Bûche de Noël

The yule log is the one Christmas dessert that is also a centerpiece.

This one is covered in chocolate ganache and pressed with shards of dark chocolate bark to read as rough tree bark, with chopped hazelnuts scattered down the sides.

A sprig of holly, a cluster of sugared red currants, and a dusting of powdered sugar sit on top, on a white platter set over two wood slices.

Tiny white ceramic houses and flocked trees fill the table behind it.

Chill the ganache until it is spreadable but not stiff. Too soft and the bark shards slide off, too firm and they crack it.

4. Make No-Bake Gingerbread Cheesecake Cups

These skip the oven entirely.

Clear cups hold a layer of crushed gingersnap crust, a thick band of cheesecake filling, and a swirl of spiced cream on top here, each one finished with a small iced gingerbread man standing upright.

A few gingersnaps and two mini candy canes sit on the counter beside them.

Press the crust in with the back of a spoon and chill for four hours minimum. The gingerbread men go in just before serving or they soften.

5. Dip Pretzels in Chocolate and Sprinkles

This is the easiest thing on the list and usually the first bowl to empty.

Pretzel twists dipped in dark and white chocolate are piled in a white ceramic bowl here, every one covered in red, green, and white nonpareils, with pine and red ornaments behind.

Dip half the batch in each chocolate. The mix of dark and white is what makes the bowl look full instead of flat.

6. Glaze Mini Chocolate Mousse Cakes

Individual cakes feel more special than a slice, and they are easier to serve.

A row of small round mousse cakes sits on a long white platter here, each with a brownie base, a glossy dark chocolate glaze, a chocolate coin topped with a white chocolate snowflake, red currants, and a silver dragée.

Fairy lights and a bit of pine blur in the background.

Set the mousse in ring molds and freeze before glazing. The glaze only goes on smooth when the cake is cold.

Place the snowflake coin at an angle rather than flat. It is what makes each one look finished.

9. Cut Peppermint Bark into Rough Squares

A layer of dark chocolate, a layer of white, and crushed candy canes pressed into the top is the whole recipe.

Thick pieces are stacked on a white plate here, the two chocolate layers visible from the side and a sprig of fresh mint on top.

Let the dark layer set until just firm before pouring the white. If it is fully hard, the two layers separate when you cut.

10. Pipe Reindeer Faces on Cupcakes

19. Build a Gingerbread House to Display, Not Eat

Pick Two Showstoppers and Fill In the Rest

Twenty-two desserts is plenty to choose from, and nobody needs to make them all.

Choose a couple of bigger bakes for the center of the table and fill in around them with the no-bake treats.

If time is short, start with the cookies, bark, and truffles.

They keep well, travel easily, and still look like real effort went in.

The simplest things here, peppermint bark and chocolate-covered pretzels, usually disappear as fast as the elaborate cakes, so not every dessert needs to be a centerpiece.

Whichever ones you choose, a table with a little variety, something chocolate, something fruity, something with sprinkles, is what makes a holiday spread feel complete.
