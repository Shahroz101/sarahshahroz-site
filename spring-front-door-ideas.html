30 Spring Front Door Ideas Sized, Hung and Matched to the Door Color

From rose wreaths on sage doors to door baskets and bows, 30 spring front door ideas with the wreath sizes, hanging heights and hooks that survive sun and wind.

2026-11-23 · 8 min read

Changing a front door for spring takes about an hour. Three decisions matter: the size of the wreath, its color against the door, and how it hangs.

Get those right and the door survives wind, sun and a week of rain. Get them wrong and the wreath looks lost or falls off by Tuesday.

These 30 ideas cover painted doors, greenery, lemons, baskets, bows and porch staging, each with a size, height or hanger detail. Read the five rules first.

Five Rules for a Spring Front Door

  • Size the wreath to the door. About two thirds of the door width, usually 22 to 26 inches, reads right from the street.
  • Hang it at 60 to 66 inches. Measure to the center of the wreath. Higher than that looks like an afterthought.
  • Choose colors from across the wheel, or a neutral. A pink door suits greens and creams, and a green door shows off pinks and yellows.
  • Choose faux for sun and rain. Real flowers brown quickly on a sunny door, and a good faux wreath lasts several seasons.
  • Use the right hanger. Over-door hooks, wired ribbon loops or magnetic hooks on steel doors beat adhesive hooks, which fail in heat.

Pair the Wreath With the Door Color

1. Put Pink on a Sage Door

A sage door is an excellent partner for pink. Choose a wreath with roses, ranunculus and a touch of lavender, as on this door.

Hang it from a loop of wired ribbon over the top of the door so the wreath does not slide.

Wire is the part people skip. Use a wired ribbon loop or a wreath hanger. Adhesive hooks fall in heat.

Check that the door still closes fully with the loop over the top.

2. Go Pale Pink on Deep Pink

A pink door with a pink wreath works when the shades differ. Use a paler pink on a deeper door, as here.

A large crock of tulips and a wicker chair keep the porch relaxed.

Set a nursery pot of tulips inside a deep crock instead of buying armfuls of cut stems.

3. Contrast a Twig Wreath With Smooth Paint

A rough twig wreath contrasts well with a smooth painted door. Add a burlap bow and a few pink blooms, as on this dusty rose door.

Black trim makes the pink look softer, so keep the rest of the decor in neutrals.

4. Let a Strong Door Take Strong Flowers

A strong door color can handle strong flowers. Orange and red tulips pop against turquoise.

Repeat the colors in a pillow and a rug so the porch feels pulled together, as here.

5. Pair a Yellow Door With a Watering Can

A yellow door sets a cheerful mood. Pair it with a galvanized can of tulips on the step.

A doormat with a simple message completes the entry.

Tuck a plastic nursery pot inside the can so the tulips have water and the metal stays dry.

Keep the wreath muted so the yellow does the talking.

6. Soften a Faded Door With Lavender

A faded paint finish suits a soft wreath. Use lavender and fern for a muted purple-and-green mix, as here.

A wicker chair with a lilac pillow beside the door repeats the purple.

Old wood doors are fragile, so hang the wreath on a wired loop and keep a hook from scratching the paint.

Size It and Hang It for the Street

7. Frame the View Through Glass

If your door has glass, let the wreath sit over the panels so it frames the view inside, as on this white door.

Look through the glass from inside. You will see the back of the wreath, so pick one that is finished on both sides, and put pots of lavender and pansies at the base to ground the display.

8. Repeat the Wreath Across the Front

Repeat the wreath across the facade and the front reads as one design. This pale blue door and the window beside it carry matching yellow-and-white wreaths.

Use the same size and hang each at the same height. Measure up from the porch floor and mark the center of each before you hang anything.

Black lanterns and a coir mat keep the base simple.

9. Choose Hydrangeas for Cream Paint

Blue hydrangeas look rich against cream paint. Add white flowers in a tall pot for balance, as here.

Hydrangea wreaths hold up better in shade. Faux ones fade slowly, so check the label for UV protection.

Center the wreath on the upper panel so it sits at eye level.

10. Scale Up for a Tall Double Door

Dark wood and white flowers look sophisticated. Use an oversized wreath, up to 30 inches, on a tall double door like this one.

Large wreaths weigh several pounds, so use a hook rated for it.

Place two lanterns with battery candles on the step and white hydrangeas to one side.

Set the lanterns on the side where the door does not swing open.

11. Try a Crescent Wreath

A crescent wreath looks modern. It uses less material and shows off the door color, as on this white door.

A potted olive tree beside the door adds a natural counterpoint.

12. Tie a Gingham Bow With Wired Ribbon

A checked bow adds a bit of farmhouse style. Tie it on with wired ribbon so the loops keep their shape.

A black-and-white mat below continues the pattern.

Greens, Lemons and Oranges

13. Hang Eucalyptus on Dark Wood

Eucalyptus stays calm against dark, weathered wood. Choose a full round wreath with small white buds, as here.

A faux eucalyptus wreath keeps its color for years and does not drop leaves.

Use an over-door hook so you do not drill into old wood.

Dust it with a soft brush once a month.

14. Use Lemons Instead of Pink

Lemon wreaths bring color without pink. They suit a natural wood door well, like this one.

Add a basket of white flowers on the step and a doormat with a simple message.

15. Pair Orange With White

Orange pairs with a white door in a fresh way. Put wooden crates of marigolds on each side.

Marigolds handle sun, which makes them a safe pick for an open stoop.

16. Give a Full Wreath a Big Ribbon

A wreath this full needs a big ribbon. A wired gingham bow adds shape and color.

Dark doors show yellow clearly. Keep the rest of the entry plain.

Stand at the curb and look. If the ribbon disappears, double its width.

17. Pair a Green Wreath With Fern Baskets

A green wreath on a wood door stays quiet. Add two big baskets of ferns and white flowers at the base.

Ferns do best in shade, so they suit a covered porch like this one.

Keep the soil damp and put saucers under the baskets.

18. Let Natural Wood Stand Alone

Let natural wood stand on its own with a plain wreath. A ribbon at the top is all it needs.

Use galvanized buckets of flowers on each side and a pair of boots for a country feel.

Punch drainage holes in the bucket bottoms or set a plastic pot inside.

Cut the ribbon loop long enough to center the wreath at eye level.

Door Baskets and Bows

19. Hang a Flat-Back Basket

A basket of flowers is quicker than a wreath. Hang a flat-back basket from an over-door hook.

Add water-filled tubes or faux flowers so the display lasts the week.

Water tubes keep real stems fresh for a few days. Faux stems last all season.

Line the back of the basket with plastic so drips do not mark the paint.

20. Hang a Basket by Its Handle

A basket with a handle looks like a gift. Hang it from the handle on a hook and keep the door otherwise plain.

Pastel tulips work best against a muted door color, like this mauve.

Use an over-door hook with a felt pad to protect the paint.

21. Keep an Evergreen Basket Year-Round

A flat basket with greenery works year-round. Hang it from an iron hook and keep the arrangement full.

Change the greens with the seasons, and the basket stays.

22. Fill a Basket With Faux Hydrangeas

Faux hydrangeas fill a basket quickly and look rich on a pale door.

Tie a gingham bow on the handle to add color, as here.

Use a hook rated for the weight of the basket.

Pale doors show dust first, so shake the basket off once a month.

23. Move the Basket Beside the Door

Hang a basket beside the door instead of on it if the door is busy. A hook on the wall works.

A striped ribbon keeps the look casual.

Fix the hook into trim or use an anchor made for your siding.

Pair it with a fern in a pot at the base, as here.

24. Tie a Burlap Bow

A big bow on a wood door is a minimalist spring option. Use wired burlap about 6 inches wide.

Place a small topiary by the door and call it done.

25. Skip the Wreath, Keep the Bow

A bow without a wreath is a clean look. Pick linen that matches the door tone.

Climbing roses around the frame, as here, supply the flowers.

Train climbing roses on wires and give them a sunny wall.

Garlands, Pots and Porch Staging

26. Wire Plastic Eggs to a Grapevine Wreath

Pastel eggs give a wreath a playful look. Make one by wiring plastic eggs to a grapevine base.

Line the steps with lanterns and set blossom branches in tall vases on each side, as here.

Use battery candles in the lanterns.

Weigh down tall vases with sand or gravel so wind does not tip them.

27. Light the Door for Evening

Lighting makes a door welcoming after dark. Hang string lights along the porch ceiling and add lanterns on the floor.

Use outdoor-rated lights and battery candles in the lanterns.

Put the lights on a timer so the entry glows at dusk without you.

28. Lean a Tall Sign Against a Heavy Pot

Leaning a tall sign beside the door adds height. Anchor it with a heavy pot so it does not tip in the wind.

Group galvanized pots of different sizes around it.

Set the pots on feet or a tray so rust does not stain the porch floor.

29. Trail a Swag Down the Doorframe

A swag along the frame is an alternative to a wreath. Attach it with zip ties and floral wire on the trim.

Choose roses and eucalyptus in muted colors so it looks natural.

Start the swag above the door and let it trail down one side, as here.

Check that nothing blocks the door from swinging.

30. Wrap the Door in a Flower Garland

A garland framing the door makes it feel like a cottage entry. Use faux flowers in pink, white and yellow.

Add a few bunny figures by the pots for a bit of whimsy.

Common Mistakes (and Fixes)

  • Mistake: a wreath too small for the door. Fix: go for at least two thirds of the door width.
  • Mistake: adhesive hooks in the sun. Fix: use an over-door hook or a wired loop.
  • Mistake: real flowers on a sunny door. Fix: use faux, or move the display to a shaded wall.
  • Mistake: ignoring wind. Fix: wire the ribbons and anchor tall items with weight.
  • Mistake: clashing with the door color. Fix: choose from across the wheel, or stay neutral.

Where to Start

Hang a wreath that fits the door at eye level and stop there if you are short on time.

When there is more time, add a mat and one big pot, and the entry reads as finished.
